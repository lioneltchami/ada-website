# Practical baking skills for displaced girls

## Project report and financial account

The PDF is the authoritative layout, including tables and photographs. This text companion preserves its evidence qualifications.

Apoti Development Association
Sharing Christ’s Love, Restoring Hope
Practical baking skills for displaced girls
A practical opportunity to begin
We brought together 15 internally displaced
teenage girls for a practical baking session in
the Sunday school hall at Full Gospel Mission,
Molyko, Buea. We wanted to offer more than
encouragement alone: an introduction to
useful skills, materials to continue practising
and modest financial support. Our aim was to
help participants explore a way of earning
through their own work, without claiming
that one session would resolve the hardships
caused by displacement.
Preparation rooted in the
community
Our team made enquiries and used word of
mouth to identify girls to invite. We obtained
the pastor’s permission to use the hall and
agreed arrangements with Auntie Dorothy,
who led the teaching. We bought ingredients
and organised the cooking setup, including
firewood, matches and pots as recalled in our
account. The financial pages distinguish
these recalled materials from separately
priced budget lines.

What happened during the session
The session lasted approximately four hours. We began
with prayer, thanks and a welcome. Dorothy encouraged
the girls about the value of being able to make useful
products themselves, then demonstrated cakes, fish pie,
meat pie and puff-puff. Toward the end, some
participants practised in front of her with guidance. This
was an introductory practical workshop, not a
multi-week course or a certified qualification.
What participants took away
Each of the 15 participants received 5,000 FCFA, a total of 75,000 FCFA in cash support.
Transparent plastic buckets were given for carrying and selling products, and remaining flour
and oil were shared for use afterward. These were remaining supplies, not a second purchase.
We do not claim identical quantities of ingredients for every girl. An honorarium was given to
the trainer; the actual amount remains to be reconciled with the plan.
What we heard afterward
Faith and dignity: Assistance was available whether or not a participant joined prayer or worship. Our reporting is
based on our retrospective account and the original written budget.

How to read the detailed schedule
The next page reproduces the original written budget amounts, not newly invented prices. The
line amounts add to 342,750 FCFA, but three lines conflict with their quantities and rates.
Participant support is confirmed separately in our account. Other budget amounts must not be
read as proof of what was paid. No receipt-backed total or independent audit is presented here.
Materials recalled but not separately priced
Firewood, matches, pots and baking soda are recalled, but the sheet has no separate prices for
them. It also does not separately identify transport or withdrawal charges. These are gaps, not
zero costs. The sheet’s kerosene and yeast entries are preserved as written; they are not silently
relabelled as firewood and baking soda.
Remaining money and accountability
Our general practice is to retain unused funds in the nonprofit’s mobile-money account for later
activities. For this project, 400,000 minus 342,750 gives 57,250, but a funding-to-budget difference
cannot establish the closing cash balance. We need the total actually spent, including operating
costs, before recording the balance remaining in our accounts. No figure here represents today’s
account balance.
No inferred cost per participant
The budget includes shared training materials, equipment and trainer support as well as
participant grants. Dividing the whole project total by 15 would not establish an identical
package value or an amount handed to each participant. The equal cash amount in our account is
5,000 FCFA per participant.
