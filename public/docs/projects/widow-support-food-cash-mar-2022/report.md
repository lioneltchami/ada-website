Care shaped by listening
On Saturday, 12 March 2022, we visited
widows in their homes in Muea, Buea,
following International Women's Day. This
was practical care expressed through our
team's time, the resources entrusted to us and
attention to what the women themselves
needed. We reached approximately 20
widows. We do not present that recalled
figure as a verified register or assume that
every woman received an identical package.
How we prepared
Our team asked around in the community and
through churches to identify women facing
particular need. We spoke with the women,
sought permission to visit and listened to
their circumstances. Our leaders brought the
information back for discussion so that we
could agree on arrangements and prepare a
relevant shopping list. This needs-led process
mattered: supplies were selected to help with
daily life, not simply to fill a standard gift bag.

Practical care and encouragement
Widows’ outreach • 12 March 2022 • Muea, Buea
What happened during the visits
We purchased supplies and organised
home-to-home delivery. The approach was to
keep supplies nearby, carry what was needed
to one home, then return for the next delivery.
We greeted each host, offered encouragement,
read the Bible and prayed before leaving the
goods. The support was goods only, not cash
assistance. Women expressed their thanks
during the visits. We do not turn those
expressions of gratitude into a claim of
measured long-term change.
What we provided
The recalled categories were African fabric wrappers (rapa), rice and other foodstuffs,
groundnut oil, red oil, salt, soap, buckets and bags. Requested pharmacy items, including eye
drops, were also part of the account. The account describes prior medical advice for those
purchases; this outreach was not a medical consultation, and this report makes no treatment or
health-outcome claim. No recipient's clinical details are disclosed.
Faith, dignity and reflection
Christian encouragement was part of our visit, not a condition for receiving help. Assistance was
available whether or not someone joined prayer or worship. Our normal practice after an
outreach is to meet as a team and discuss what went well, timing and what should improve. No
project-specific debrief minutes or measured follow-up outcomes are presented here.
